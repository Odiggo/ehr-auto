Doctor: Hi, come on in. What seems to be troubling you today?
Patient: Hi Doctor. For the past five days, I’ve been having this really uncomfortable bloating in my stomach. It feels like I’m full of air, even when I haven’t eaten much.

Doctor: I see. When did you first notice the bloating?
Patient: It started maybe last Thursday. At first it was mild—just a little pressure. But now it’s happening after almost every meal, even small ones.

Doctor: Is the bloating constant, or does it come and go?
Patient: It comes and goes, but usually worse in the afternoon and evening. Mornings are a bit better.

Doctor: Any nausea, vomiting, or abdominal pain?
Patient: No vomiting. I did feel slightly nauseous twice, but not enough to throw up. I wouldn’t call it pain—more like pressure and discomfort.

Doctor: Any diarrhea or constipation?
Patient: Constipation, actually. I’ve only had a couple of bowel movements in the past four days. They were small and hard.

Doctor: Any changes in your diet recently—new foods, more dairy, spicy foods, high-fat meals?
Patient: Well, I’ve been eating more takeout lately. Work got busy and I haven’t been cooking. Lots of sandwiches, pizza, and fast meals.

Doctor: That kind of diet can definitely affect digestion. Have you been drinking enough water?
Patient: Probably not. Mostly coffee during the day and maybe a soda or two.

Doctor: That may contribute to the constipation and bloating. Have you noticed if certain foods trigger the bloating more?
Patient: Yeah—bread and pasta especially. Sometimes dairy too.

Doctor: Any history of food intolerances, like lactose intolerance?
Patient: Not officially, but I’ve sometimes suspected dairy might bother me.

Doctor: Any fever, blood in your stool, or weight loss?
Patient: No fever, no blood, and no weight loss.

Doctor: How stressed have you been lately?
Patient: Pretty stressed. Work deadlines, not sleeping much, eating on the go.

Doctor: Stress can also slow digestion and cause bloating and constipation.

Patient: That makes sense. I didn’t think about that.

Doctor: Have you taken any medications recently—antibiotics, painkillers, supplements?
Patient: I took ibuprofen a few times last week for a headache. Other than that, nothing.

Doctor: Any abdominal surgeries in the past?
Patient: Nope.

Doctor: Alright, let me examine your abdomen.
Patient: Okay.

Doctor: (Examining) Your abdomen is mildly distended, but soft. No masses, no tenderness, and bowel sounds are present. This is consistent with functional bloating and constipation, likely from a combination of diet changes, dehydration, and stress.

Patient: So nothing serious?

Doctor: No signs of anything dangerous. Most likely you’re experiencing constipation-related bloating, sometimes called slow-transit constipation. When stool builds up in the colon, gas builds up too, causing that pressure and fullness.

Patient: That definitely feels like what’s happening.

Doctor: Let’s talk about treatment. I recommend a three-part approach.

Patient: Okay, I’m listening.

Doctor: First, hydration. Increase water intake to 6–8 glasses a day. Coffee and soda don’t count—they can dehydrate you.

Patient: I can do that.

Doctor: Second, dietary changes for the next week:
	•	Add more fiber—fruits, vegetables, oats, whole grains
	•	Cut down processed foods
	•	Avoid large portions of bread and pasta temporarily
	•	Avoid carbonated drinks, which add gas

Patient: Got it.

Doctor: Third, medication. I recommend starting polyethylene glycol (MiraLAX). It’s a gentle osmotic laxative that helps draw water into the intestines to soften stool. Take 17 grams (one capful) in a glass of water once daily for 5–7 days.

Patient: Will it make me run to the bathroom?

Doctor: It’s usually very gentle. It softens things gradually, not suddenly. Most people tolerate it well. If stools get too loose, just cut the dose in half.

Patient: Okay, that sounds manageable.

Doctor: You can also use simethicone (Gas-X) as needed for gas pressure; it helps break up gas bubbles.

Patient: I’ve heard of that one. I can pick it up today.

Doctor: In addition, taking a probiotic daily for the next couple of weeks may help regulate digestion and reduce bloating. Something like Lactobacillus or Bifidobacterium strains.

Patient: Sure, I can try that.

Doctor: Finally, light movement helps digestion. A daily 15–20 minute walk can really make a difference.

Patient: I definitely haven’t been moving much lately.

Doctor: That’s very common when people get busy. But movement stimulates the intestines to work more efficiently.

Patient: Okay. So increase water, take MiraLAX, eat better, move more.

Doctor: Exactly. I’d like you to follow this plan and check back with me in 7–10 days. By then, the bloating should be much better.

Patient: And if it isn’t?

Doctor: If symptoms persist after a week, we can explore additional tests such as:
	•	Bloodwork for anemia, thyroid issues
	•	Testing for celiac disease
	•	Stool studies
	•	And if the symptoms linger beyond several weeks, possibly an abdominal ultrasound

But I don’t think you’ll need those. Your symptoms match constipation-related bloating very clearly.

Patient: That makes me feel better.

Doctor: Good. Also, contact me sooner if you develop:
	•	Severe abdominal pain
	•	Fever
	•	Vomiting
	•	Inability to pass gas or stool
	•	Blood in the stool

Patient: Understood.

Doctor: So I’ll send in the MiraLAX recommendation to your chart, but you can buy it over the counter. Start tonight, drink plenty of water, and begin the dietary changes tomorrow.

Patient: Will do. Thanks for all the help.

Doctor: You’re welcome. You should feel improvement within a few days, and I’ll see you in about a week for follow-up.

Patient: Sounds good. Thank you, Doctor.
