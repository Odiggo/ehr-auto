Patient: Yes, my shoulders have felt really tight. And my partner told me I sometimes clench my jaw at night.

Doctor: That fits well with tension-type headaches. They often feel like a “band” of pressure around the head and are linked with muscle tension, poor sleep, and stress.

Patient: That sounds exactly like what I’m feeling.

Doctor: Have you taken anything for the headache—ibuprofen, acetaminophen, anything like that?
Patient: I’ve taken ibuprofen maybe twice. It helped a bit, but the headache came back later.

Patient: I’ve been drinking more coffee than usual—like three or four cups a day—to stay awake with all the work.

Doctor: Caffeine can help headaches in small amounts, but too much or inconsistent intake can actually make them worse.

Patient: That might be part of it then.

Doctor: Let me check your vital signs and do a quick exam.
Patient: Okay.

Doctor: Your blood pressure looks fine, your neuro exam is normal, and the muscles in your neck and shoulders are very tight. That supports the diagnosis of a tension headache rather than something more serious.

Patient: That’s reassuring. I was worried it might be something serious.

Doctor: Understandable. But your symptoms don’t fit dangerous causes. Now let’s talk about treatment. The good news is tension headaches respond well to simple measures.

Patient: Great, I’m ready to try anything.

Doctor: First, for medication:
